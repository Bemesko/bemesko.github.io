## The problem

The client's main subscription was costing around **US$ 40k per month**. They wanted that down without buying extra hours, and the decision to continue the partnership was a few months away.

## The approach

With a colleague I ran the diagnosis and wrote the methodology, then went straight into fortnightly sprints without a separate discovery phase. The workstreams:

- **Rightsizing** VMs and SQL, dropping high availability from dev and QA.
- **Storage** redundancy and tiers.
- **Proxies** consolidated from 9 VMs to 6, keeping the agreed 2% tolerance for failed requests.
- **AKS** moved to a region in Brazil.
- **Tags and policies** to surface idle resources.

I executed the Azure DevOps licence clean-up, the container registry clean-up and RBAC on AKS myself. Not everything paid off: a SQL elastic pool looked promising and didn't, so we dropped it.

## Results

| Workstream | Reported savings / month |
| ---------- | ------------------------ |
| AKS        | ~US$ 9k                  |
| Storage    | ~US$ 6k                  |
| SQL        | ~US$ 3k                  |
| VMs        | ~US$ 2k                  |
| **Total**  | **over US$ 20k**         |

That's **about 20% above the target**, as reported in the PMO status. The client decided to stay, which kept both the account and its Azure consumption.

## Lessons

Cost work touches production. The registry retention we introduced here later removed images that production still needed; the post-mortem led to separate production tags, retention reviews and alerts on image pull failures.
